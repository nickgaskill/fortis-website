How much does a kitchen remodel actually cost?

The honest answer is "it depends." Here's exactly what it depends on, so you can tell where your project will land before you ever ask for a bid.

Every homeowner wants a number before they'll pick up the phone. That's fair — but a straight dollar figure for "a kitchen remodel" is close to meaningless, because a kitchen remodel isn't one project. It's three or four different projects that happen to share a name, and which one you're actually doing is what sets your budget.

Start with scope, not a dollar figure

Think of kitchen projects in three tiers. A cosmetic refresh keeps every wall, cabinet box, and plumbing line exactly where it is — new paint, hardware, a countertop swap, maybe a backsplash. A mid-range remodel replaces cabinets and counters and updates lighting and appliances, but generally keeps the room's footprint. A full gut renovation moves walls, relocates plumbing and gas lines, and sometimes changes the room's footprint entirely. Each tier costs meaningfully more than the one before it — not because materials get more expensive in a straight line, but because the invisible work stacks up. A gut renovation means paying for demo, rough plumbing, and electrical before a single cabinet ever gets installed.

What pushes the number up

A handful of decisions do most of the work of moving your budget: moving or removing a load-bearing wall, relocating a sink or gas line to a different wall, upgrading an older electrical panel to support new appliances, and the cabinetry tier you choose — stock, semi-custom, or fully custom all carry very different price points. Countertop material matters too; laminate, quartz, and natural stone sit at different ends of the spectrum. And in a lot of Central Texas neighborhoods with older housing stock, there's a wildcard: what's actually behind the wall. Outdated wiring, undersized plumbing, or framing that doesn't match the original permit are common finds once demo starts, and they change the scope mid-project if they're not caught early.

What keeps it in check

The single biggest lever is keeping your plumbing and gas lines where they already are — moving the sink six feet sounds simple and rarely is. Beyond that, reusing sound cabinet boxes and refacing rather than replacing them, phasing work if your timeline allows it, and locking in your selections (cabinets, counters, tile, hardware) before demo starts all keep a project moving instead of stalling out on change orders. Indecision mid-project is one of the most common — and most avoidable — sources of delay and added cost.

Why three quotes rarely tell you three real numbers

"Get three bids and go with the one in the middle" only works if all three bids are pricing the same scope with the same materials — and most aren't. A lower number often means lower allowances for cabinets and counters that get exceeded the moment you pick real finishes, or it means separate subs for demo, plumbing, electrical, and cabinetry with no one accountable for how the pieces fit together. We run every project as one line-item estimate before work begins, with every trade under one roof and one point of contact from first walkthrough to final punch list — so the number you're quoted is the number the project actually costs.

The only way to get a real number for your kitchen is to walk it. If you're deciding between a refresh and a full renovation, our kitchen remodeling page covers what's included at each level, and interior remodels covers how a kitchen fits into a larger renovation if you're touching more than one room. From there, a free consultation is the fastest way to turn "it depends" into an actual number for your house.
