How to choose a general contractor in Austin

The right contractor is the difference between a project that goes smoothly and one that drags on for twice as long as it should. Here's what to actually check before you sign.

Austin's building boom means homeowners have no shortage of contractors to choose from — which sounds like a good thing until you're the one trying to tell them apart. Glossy websites and confident sales pitches don't tell you who shows up on time, who answers the phone mid-project, or who's still in business to honor a warranty two years from now. Here's what actually separates a good hire from a headache.

Confirm the license and insurance — don't just take their word for it

Texas doesn't require a statewide general contractor license, which means anyone can call themselves one. That makes insurance your real safety net: ask for proof of general liability coverage and workers' compensation, and confirm the policy is current, not expired. If a contractor hesitates to produce documentation or brushes off the question, that's your answer. A legitimate operation shares this without friction because they carry it for exactly this reason.

Ask who you'll actually talk to

On a lot of jobs, the person who sells the project isn't the person who runs it, and the person who runs it isn't always easy to reach once work starts. Before you sign anything, ask directly: who is my point of contact once the crew is on-site, and how do I reach them? A contractor who gives you a clear, single answer — one name, one number — is telling you something about how the whole project will run. One who shrugs is telling you something too.

Look for a real, written process — not just a bid

A low number on a one-page estimate is easy to produce and hard to trust. What you want is a process: an initial conversation about your goals and budget, a design phase that ends in a detailed, line-item estimate and a schedule, then a build phase with regular updates and inspections along the way. If a contractor can't describe how they move a project from a conversation to a finished space, they probably don't have a repeatable one — and that unpredictability becomes your problem the moment the project starts. Our own design-build process is built around exactly this kind of structure, and it's worth asking any contractor you're considering to walk you through theirs in the same detail.

Read reviews for patterns, not just star ratings

Anyone can collect a handful of five-star reviews. What matters more is whether the same specific things come up across multiple reviews — clear communication, crews that showed up when promised, a final product that matched what was discussed upfront. Those repeated, specific details are far more telling than an average score, and they're also the easiest thing to fake with a vague testimonial. Look for names, dates, and details you could actually verify if you wanted to.

Watch how they handle the budget conversation

Every remodel or build runs into a decision point where quality, scope, and cost pull against each other — a plumbing surprise behind an old wall, a material that's back-ordered, a change of heart on a layout. A contractor worth hiring talks about that possibility honestly before it happens, rather than promising a number that can't survive contact with an older Austin house. Ask how they handle change orders and unexpected findings, and get the answer in writing.

Trust the in-person consultation

Bids and websites only tell you so much. The in-home consultation is where you actually learn how a contractor listens, how they think through your space, and whether they're straight with you about tradeoffs. Notice whether they ask questions about how you live in your home, or just measure and move on. That first meeting is a good preview of every meeting after it.

None of this is complicated, but it does take a little discipline to check before you're excited about a bid. A free consultation is the easiest place to start — reach out and see how the conversation goes.
